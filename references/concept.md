# Concept: from message to idea

A designer who skips this step makes "a nice slide". An art director makes a post that someone remembers.
The order is always: **objective → single message → insight → idea → execution**. Execution (fonts,
colours) is the last decision, not the first.

## 1. Single message and insight
- **Single message**: one sentence a stranger could repeat after one look. If you need "and", you have two posts.
  Bad: "We offer premium plots with great amenities and flexible payment." Good: "Land here will cost more next year."
- **Insight**: the human truth or tension the message rides on. Ask: what does the audience believe now, what
  do we want them to believe, what is in the way? The gap is the insight.
  e.g. Real estate buyers think "luxury = big house" → truth: "the rich buy what can't be recreated: land, views, scarcity."
- **Proof**: what makes it believable (a number, a receipt, a before/after, a name, a demonstration).
  A post with an insight and proof gets saved; a post with only adjectives gets scrolled.

## 2. Idea generators
Run several; keep the one that is simplest and most ownable.
1. **Dramatise the benefit**: show the result at its most extreme (a calendar with every weekend free).
2. **Dramatise the problem**: show the pain so precisely it hurts (a receipt of hidden costs).
3. **Visual metaphor**: say X by showing Y (see §3).
4. **Borrowed format**: present the content as something people already know how to read: a receipt,
   boarding pass, menu, ticket, map, prescription, report card, chat screenshot, notification, recipe,
   label, warning sign, contract clause, film one-sheet, magazine cover, spreadsheet, search results.
5. **Juxtaposition / contrast**: then vs now, expectation vs reality, them vs us, cheap vs expensive, myth vs fact.
6. **Number as hero**: one huge number with a small line that makes it surprising ("2,000 children. 10 years.").
7. **Type is the image**: the words themselves form the picture (stacked, cropped, rotated, filled with photo).
8. **Exaggeration / scale**: tiny product in a vast field, or a detail blown up to fill the frame (macro).
9. **Absence**: show what's missing (an empty chair, a blank space where a feature should be).
10. **Series device**: a repeating character, frame, colour per episode, "Part 1/Part 2", numbered field notes.
11. **Wordplay that the picture completes**: a line that only makes sense with the image (and vice versa).
12. **Cultural moment**: a festival, season, news beat or meme format re-cut in the brand's language
    (only if the brand has standing to join it).

## 3. Visual metaphor
Academic typology (Phillips & McQuarrie; van Mulken et al.), from easiest to hardest to read:
- **Juxtaposition**: two things side by side; the viewer compares. Easiest to read; strong for carousels
  (slide A vs slide B) and split layouts.
- **Fusion**: two things merged into one object (a building whose windows are calendar squares). Arresting,
  harder to read; keep everything else in the frame silent.
- **Replacement**: one thing stands in for an absent other (a huge dog with a tiny bowl = more power, less fuel).
  Most rewarding when decoded, most risky; the context must point to the answer.
- **Hyperbole**: impossible scale or quantity to make a quality felt.
Rules: the metaphor must be decodable in 2 seconds by the actual audience; the brand/product must be one side
of the comparison; never stack two metaphors; if you must explain it in the caption, it failed.
For AI-image generation, describe the metaphor as a photographable scene, not as a concept.

## 4. Cover hooks (slide 1 / single static headline)
The cover has one job: earn the swipe or the stop. Patterns that work (all seen in the study):
- **Question that implies a shift**: "Is the next generation changing the address game?"
- **Numbered promise**: "5 signs the land title is fake" (+ "Part 1" for series).
- **How-X-does-Y**: "How billionaires choose real estate".
- **Definition gap**: "What exactly is an Aerotropolis?"
- **Contrarian**: "Stop posting tips. Post decisions."
- **Specific outcome**: "From 0 to 100M organic views a month."
- **Name the enemy**: "The hidden cost nobody puts in the brochure."
- **Image-only cover** with a 1–3 word title when the picture is the hook (film, food, product drops).
Keep cover headlines ≤ ~10 words; the most important 2–4 words get the emphasis device.
Slide 2 is a second cover: people who saw slide 1 before and scrolled past get shown slide 2 by Instagram,
so it must also work alone.

## 5. Carousel story arcs
Pick one; write one line per slide before designing.
1. **Problem → Agitate → Solve → Proof → CTA** (sales/education).
2. **Myth ⇄ Fact pairs** (cover with tension, then 3–5 myth/fact pairs, CTA) — drives comments.
3. **Listicle with a payoff**: hook, N items (one per slide), summary formula slide ("A + B + C = D"), CTA. Saves.
4. **Step-by-step / how-to**: outcome first, steps, common mistake, recap. Saves.
5. **Before → After → How**: transformation first, then method.
6. **Shift**: "what used to matter" vs "what matters now", one dimension per slide, verdict.
7. **Question → reasons → answer** (Guardians pattern): open question, 4 shifts with a takeaway pill each, payoff.
8. **Story / case**: setup, conflict, turn, result, lesson.
9. **Teaser / curiosity gap**: bold claim, two reveals, cliffhanger to caption/link/Part 2.
10. **Showcase / menu** (Katha pattern): hero ↔ detail rhythm, one item per pair, bookend with brand.
11. **Event walk-through** (AM-PM pattern): what, then each world/zone, then date-place-tickets countdown.
12. **Proof wall**: claim, three receipts (numbers, quotes, logos), offer.
Length: as long as each slide earns its place; 5–10 is typical. If a slide could be cut without loss, cut it.
Each slide should end on something quotable (a takeaway line, a pill, a number).

## 6. Single-static formats
Announcement (launch, partnership, opening) · offer/price · event poster · speaker/host card (four-corner) ·
quote card · testimonial · stat card · thank-you/milestone · hiring · festival greeting · product hero ·
before/after split · meme/format remix · infographic snapshot · teaser ("soon").
A single static needs **one** focal point and at most three text levels. Festival greetings should carry a
brand idea, not just "Happy Diwali" on a stock diya: tie the festival to what the brand actually does.

## 7. Test before designing
- Say the single message out loud; then describe the cover in one sentence. Do they match?
- Would the target audience save, send or comment? Which one, and why?
- Could a competitor post this unchanged? If yes, make it more specific to the brand (their product, place,
  people, numbers, voice, motif).
- Is anything claimed that you can't support? Remove or mark it as an open item.
